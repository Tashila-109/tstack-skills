### Autopilot-full

**A coordinator runs this program; it is not a lead's playbook.** For "autopilot this queue", "full autopilot", and queues of independent PRs the human wants merged without being asked each time. Each queue item is its own task with its own lead, who carries it from build to a verified, merge-ready PR. The coordinator keeps the verdict audit and the merges, under one standing grant from the human. [Orchestrate](orchestrate.md) is different: one lead runs one program, and nothing merges without the human's word each time.

1. **Confirm the grant and honor state-then-wait.** Before anything starts, ask the human once: "I will merge each PR in this queue as soon as its verification is clean, without asking again. Which items, if any, stay for you to merge?" Record their answer, in their words, in `<home>/autopilot-<name>.md`, with the queue. That answer is the standing merge grant for exactly those items, and for nothing else. Items the human keeps stop at merge-ready. When the human asks for the plan first, state it and stop; start only on their explicit go.
2. **Brief one task per item.** Pick each item's playbook from the intake table, with `pr` delivery. Add to each brief's goal: open the PR ready, never draft, as soon as the first verified stage is committed; babysit it in `drive` mode to merge-ready; and report merge-ready with the head SHA.
3. **Run leads in true parallel, never stacked.** Launch leads at once when items are self-contained: one task branch per lead, disjoint files, drift absorbed by bringing the default branch in. Only genuinely overlapping work waits: launch the second lead when the first has merged.
4. **Audit each merge-ready report before the merge.** A lead's PR is merge-ready only with independent verification on its reported head: the final review that [Opening a PR](opening-a-pr.md) requires, by a reviewer who did not write the code, and a live lane on the real surface the change touches, delegated on the `verify` seat, plus a regression lane against the default branch. Run `tstack.mjs check <id>`, then read the evidence. The verified head SHA must be the PR's current head, or its patch-id must match the verified one, as [Shipping](shipping.md#landing) checks. A finding a lane filed as a note is still a finding. Send every proven finding back to the lead in one message, and wait for a fresh merge-ready report.
5. **Merge on a clean audit, under the grant.** Land each verified PR as [Shipping's Landing](shipping.md#landing) says, one at a time. Then clean up as the runtime reference says, read the report's `## Remember` and `## Follow-ups`, and launch the lead for the next item that was waiting. Items the human kept stop at merge-ready: give the human the PR's full URL.
6. **Keep checking on the program.** On T3, arm `schedule_task` for an hourly check-in on the coordinator's own thread when the queue starts, and delete it when the queue ends. On Herdr, check every lead each time you wake. At each check, re-read this playbook, look at every lead's state and its pushed branch, and count only side effects as progress: commits, pushes, and PR or check changes. A lead that has made no progress for longer than its work should take gets a look: read its thread or pane, and tell the human what you found. Never restart a lead on a guess.
7. **Stand down at once on the human's stop.** Hold every running lead, as dispatch's hold verb says, and delete the hourly check-in. Release each lead only on the human's word.

**Reply:** the queue with each PR's lead, state and head SHA; each audit and what it found; what merged, and which lead took the next item; open items waiting for the human; and where the grant is recorded.
